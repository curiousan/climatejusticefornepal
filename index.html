The August 2026 floods

The climate debt
owed to Nepal

On 26 August 2026, a collapse of rock and glacier ice unleashed devastating floods in Nepal. Rising temperatures melt ice and thaw frozen ground, weakening mountain slopes and increasing the risk of such disasters.

Nepal contributed only about 0.01% of historical global CO₂ emissions, yet its communities face catastrophic losses. This is the case for climate debt: countries most responsible for warming should help pay for recovery, compensate affected communities, and fund protection against future disasters.

Why this is climate injustice

The burden is not
shared equally.

Nepal’s historical contribution to global CO₂ emissions is extremely small. Its communities nevertheless face severe losses from floods and other climate-related hazards.

Emergency relief is only the beginning. Recovery also means rebuilding homes and public services, supporting displaced families, and preparing communities for future risks.

Sources & transparency

Facts with context.

These are dated reports, not a real-time casualty count. Estimates can change as assessments continue.

Reported deaths / remains · 1,411 · 19 Sep 2026
Official recovery tally includes 526 entries for human remains; it is not a count of individually identified victims. NDRRMA / Ministry of Finance

People reported missing · 5,875 · 19 Sep 2026
May overlap with unidentified remains, rescued people or duplicate entries. Do not add this figure to the death tally. NDRRMA / Ministry of Finance

Injured treated by security teams · 10,041 · 19 Sep 2026
Security-agency treatment figure. Separately, 18 people were in hospital. The latest report does not provide a complete cumulative injury total; these categories are not added together. NDRRMA / Ministry of Finance

People rescued · 13,784 · 19 Sep 2026
Reported rescues; this is not a count of people currently displaced. NDRRMA / Ministry of Finance

Private buildings damaged · 7,570 · 13 Sep 2026
Government RDNA, summarized by Nepal Red Cross on 13 September. Counts damaged private buildings, not only destroyed homes. Replaces the earlier approximate housing figure; definitions differ. Nepal Red Cross / RDNA

Estimated physical damage · $1.80B · 11 Sep 2026
Rounded government estimate of physical damage, separate from economic losses and reconstruction needs. A fuller post-disaster assessment is still pending. Government of Nepal / MoFA

Estimated economic losses · $883M · 11 Sep 2026
Rounded RDNA loss estimate. This is separate from damage to physical assets. Government of Nepal / MoFA

Recovery & reconstruction · $4.78B · 11 Sep 2026
Rounded government estimate of reconstruction requirements. It is not an additional damage amount and must not be added to damage and losses. Government of Nepal / MoFA

Flood trigger · 9 September 2026
Associated Press reports that a high-altitude collapse of bedrock and glacier ice sent debris and water downstream. Rising temperatures melt stabilizing ice and thaw frozen ground, making steep mountain slopes less stable and destructive collapses more likely. Associated Press explanation ↗

China’s annual rate and Nepal’s historical total
China emitted about 12.3 billion tonnes in 2024. Nepal’s cumulative total through 2024 was about 232 million tonnes. At that average rate, China emits the same amount in 366 × 232 million ÷ 12.3 billion ≈ 6.9 days.
